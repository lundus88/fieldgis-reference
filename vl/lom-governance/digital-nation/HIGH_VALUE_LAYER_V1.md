# LOM Virtual World — High-Value Layer v1

Status: DRAFT / NON-PRODUCTION  
Date: 2026-09-29  
Owner: LOM Core Governance

## Objective

Add only capabilities that materially increase member utility, economic activity, trust, retention, defensibility, recurring revenue, or enterprise value.

Rule:

**reuse -> extend -> integrate -> build only on proven gap**

No capability in this layer may create a duplicate CRM, LMS, payment processor, accounting engine, identity authority, reputation engine, dispute engine, HRMS, or regulated financial institution.

## High-Value Capabilities

### 0. LOM Market
The unified market surface of LOM Virtual World.

It combines:
- services;
- work/gigs;
- digital goods/IP;
- B2B demand/supply;
- virtual assets.

Canonical owner: LD Commerce, with LOM Trust owning disputes/reputation boundaries and LOM Core owning policy/authority.

P0 front door:
- Hire a Service
- Find Work
- Buy Digital Products
- Start / Grow a Business

High value:
- creates one visible economic center for the Virtual World;
- increases buyer/seller liquidity;
- connects learning, work, business and commerce;
- gives the Golden World Transaction a real customer-facing destination.

It reuses existing marketplace, commerce, payment, opportunity and trust capabilities and must not become a duplicate commerce engine.


### 1. Member Economic Passport
A private platform profile that combines:
- LOM Member ID;
- verified skills;
- work history;
- accepted deliveries;
- business roles;
- platform entitlements;
- evidence-backed reputation signals;
- learning history;
- transaction references.

It is not legal citizenship, a passport, a government identity, a credit report, or a statutory licence.

High value:
- reduces repeated onboarding;
- improves matching;
- builds portable trust inside LOM;
- increases switching cost through useful accumulated history.

### 2. Skill-to-Income Graph
Connect:

Learning -> Verified Skill -> Opportunity -> Work -> Evidence -> Earnings -> Next Skill.

The graph must explain why an opportunity is recommended and must not hide adverse decisions behind an opaque score.

High value:
- turns education into economic outcomes;
- creates a closed loop between LOM Education and Economic Participation;
- provides measurable time-to-income.

### 3. AI Personal Economic Agent
Each member may have a bounded assistant that can:
- identify suitable learning;
- prepare applications/proposals;
- surface jobs/gigs/business opportunities;
- organize tasks;
- explain earnings and costs;
- recommend next steps;
- prepare drafts for human review.

It may not:
- bind the member to material contracts without authority;
- spend, borrow, invest, or transfer money without explicit authority;
- misrepresent credentials;
- make irreversible high-impact decisions.

High value:
- daily engagement;
- personalized navigation of the entire ecosystem;
- lower friction from intent to outcome.

### 4. Opportunity Exchange
Unified surface for:
- jobs;
- gigs;
- project work;
- creator work;
- affiliate opportunities;
- business-to-business requests;
- lawful tenders/opportunities.

Sources should reuse LundusLead and Economic Participation discovery/matching capability.

High value:
- network effect;
- increases frequency of useful member interactions;
- creates demand-side liquidity for the ecosystem.

### 5. Business-in-a-Box
A member can create a platform business workspace containing:
- profile;
- catalogue;
- service/product offer;
- quotation;
- order;
- delivery;
- accounting adapter;
- customer support;
- analytics.

This is a platform workspace, not legal incorporation or statutory business registration.

High value:
- converts workers into sellers;
- raises ARPU;
- increases recurring commercial activity.

### 6. Evidence Wallet
Private member-controlled evidence store for:
- skill evidence;
- learning certificates;
- delivery acceptance;
- business documents;
- transaction references;
- reputation evidence;
- approved credentials.

High value:
- reduces trust friction;
- allows reusable verified evidence;
- strengthens auditability and portability.

Privacy rule:
Only minimum necessary evidence should be disclosed to another party.

### 7. Trust Passport
A human-readable trust view derived from evidence dimensions, not one opaque global score.

Possible dimensions:
- delivery reliability;
- verified skill;
- transaction integrity;
- policy compliance;
- dispute outcomes where materially relevant.

High value:
- lowers marketplace risk;
- improves repeat transactions;
- preserves explainability.

No human-worth score. No automatic irreversible sanction.

### 8. Creator & Digital IP Economy
Allow lawful digital assets such as:
- templates;
- software;
- designs;
- educational content;
- datasets where rights permit;
- digital services;
- licensed virtual assets.

Core controls:
- provenance;
- rights/ownership declarations;
- licence terms;
- versioning;
- delivery evidence;
- refund/dispute handling.

High value:
- scalable inventory with low marginal cost;
- recurring and global revenue potential.

### 9. Virtual Business Spaces
Members/businesses may own or rent governed digital spaces:
- storefront;
- office;
- classroom;
- project room;
- event space;
- showroom.

These are platform entitlements only and never physical land title.

High value:
- persistent identity and presence;
- additional monetization surface;
- stronger ecosystem attachment.

### 10. Economic Mobility Engine
Measure member progress using factual indicators such as:
- verified skills gained;
- opportunities qualified;
- completed work;
- earnings;
- business activity;
- repeat customers;
- learning progression.

The engine should recommend next actions, not assign a social class or human value.

High value:
- connects platform use to measurable personal progress;
- supports retention and upsell based on actual needs.

### 11. Member-to-Member Services
Enable governed peer commerce:
- tutoring;
- design;
- writing;
- coding;
- surveying-related digital support where lawful;
- consulting;
- remote assistance;
- digital production.

High value:
- increases internal economic velocity;
- converts members into both buyers and sellers.

### 12. Global Commerce Bridge
Adapters for:
- multi-currency pricing;
- lawful payment providers;
- invoicing/accounting;
- international delivery;
- tax/compliance handoff;
- language/localization.

High value:
- lets one successful product or service reach multiple markets.

Regulated functions stay with licensed providers.

### 13. Reputation-to-Opportunity, Not Reputation-to-Punishment
Use verified evidence to improve matching and reduce friction.

Rules:
- adverse signals are contextual;
- dispute opening alone is not wrongdoing;
- explain material decisions;
- allow correction/appeal;
- avoid permanent exclusion from weak evidence.

High value:
- preserves trust without creating a punitive social-credit system.

### 14. Economic Safety Net Features
Platform-level support may include:
- learning credits;
- promotional credits;
- hardship fee waivers;
- grants funded by LOM;
- retraining pathways;
- opportunity re-matching.

These are company/platform programmes, not state welfare or social security.

High value:
- reduces churn after temporary failure;
- preserves productive members;
- aligns ecosystem growth with member progress.

### 15. Virtual World Economic Twin
A privacy-preserving aggregate model of:
- member activity;
- business activity;
- opportunity demand;
- skill shortages;
- transaction volume;
- delivery capacity;
- disputes;
- retention.

High value:
- enables capacity planning;
- identifies missing products and skills;
- supports Product Factory prioritization;
- improves resource allocation.

It must not expose private member data or fabricate economic statistics.

## North Star Metrics

Measure:
- activated members;
- time to first useful outcome;
- time to first verified skill;
- time to first qualified opportunity;
- time to first earning;
- member earnings;
- repeat transaction rate;
- business creation/activity;
- learning-to-work conversion;
- buyer/seller retention;
- dispute/refund rate;
- LOM recurring revenue;
- contribution margin.

Do not use vanity population numbers as the primary success metric.

## Boundary

The Virtual World should become more valuable because members can **learn, work, earn, build, trade, prove trust, and progress** inside one governed ecosystem.

It should not become valuable by imitating sovereign powers it does not possess.


## Society-critical high-value additions

The economic layer alone is insufficient for a credible Virtual World. The following cross-cutting capabilities are now treated as high-value composition priorities:

- **World Search & Navigation** — one route to people, businesses, services, work, learning, products, events and virtual places.
- **Privacy & Data Control Center** — member control over visibility, consent, evidence sharing and connected services.
- **Member Service Center** — one place for support, recovery, transaction help, disputes and domain routing.
- **Personal Home / Dashboard** — a private composition of tasks, learning, opportunities, orders, earnings evidence, businesses, events and alerts.
- **Community & Groups** — bounded social/professional communities with moderation and trust controls.
- **Member Voice & Transparency** — feedback, non-binding proposals, change logs, explanations and appeal routes under founder-led governance.
- **Developer & Partner Platform** — controlled APIs/adapters so the ecosystem can expand without LOM rebuilding every external service.

Detailed controls and sequencing are defined in `SOCIETY_ESSENTIALS_V1.md`.
